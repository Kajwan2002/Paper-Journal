§ 01

The one principle

Everything else is downstream of this sentence.

The page always looks analog. The intelligence lives in the margins, the transitions, and the things that happen while you're not looking.

A leather notebook can't search itself, can't nudge you, can't carry an unfinished task to tomorrow. Those are the only reasons to build this as software. But the smart parts should feel like the notebook quietly did them for you — never like an app was bolted onto a photo of paper.

The test for any feature: if it needs a settings-heavy panel, a modal stack, or a notification-center feel, it probably breaks the illusion and should be cut or hidden.

Borrowed from bullet journalling and used as the visual grammar throughout the app — you type these at the start of a line and the page understands them.

§ 02

Feature set — three tiers

Ranked by priority, because the ranking is real: Tier 1 is the product, Tier 2 makes it a planner, Tier 3 is the part you can live without.

Tier 1 — the core loop

  • The daily page

    Home screen. Pre-printed date header and page number, a morning-intentions block at the top, open space, an evening-reflection prompt at the bottom. Modelled on the Hobonichi Techo one-page-a-day rhythm.

  • Natural-language capture

    Type "gym mon/wed/fri 7am" or "call mum sunday" and it silently becomes a recurring block or a reminder — still shown as your text on the line, with a hairline underline marking it as "live."

  • Task rollover (migration)

    An unchecked task draws a "›" and reappears on tomorrow's page. You can see the drift — something that's rolled five times looks nagged, on purpose.

  • Real calendar, handwritten

    Two-way sync with Google, iCloud and Outlook over CalDAV. Appointments appear as ink on a margin timeline, not as blocks in a grid.

  • Search across every page

    The one thing paper fundamentally can't do. A magnifying-glass ribbon; results shown as page-corner peeks you flip straight to.

Tier 2 — the planning layer

  • Weekly & monthly spreads

    Auto-populated from daily pages and the calendar, but still hand-editable — drop a note in a corner, cross a day out.

  • Margin-rail time-blocking

    Drag a task onto the day's vertical timeline to give it a slot. The rail is the same one the calendar draws on.

  • Habit & mood grid

    A monthly dot grid you fill in by tapping. Trends surface quietly — a faint trend line, a margin note at month's end.

  • Collections

    Tag a line with a symbol and a named collection assembles itself across pages — a project, a trip, a reading list. This is the Leuchtturm index, made automatic.

  • On this day

    A ribbon that opens to what you wrote a month or a year ago today.

Tier 3 — gentle intelligence

  • End-of-week reflection

    A short note in the week's margin: "You moved 'taxes' four times this week." Observational, not prescriptive.

  • Prompts on blank days

    A faint pencil line of a question when a page has been left empty and it's late.

  • Draft my week

    Proposes a plan from your open tasks and calendar; you accept it by editing it, line by line.

Deliberately out of scope

To protect the feel, these are not coming — at least not in v1.

  • Collaboration / shared notebooks. A journal is a single-author object.
  • Project-management boards. No Kanban, no Gantt, no assignees.
  • A notification firehose. At most one gentle daily nudge.
  • Infinite nested folders. Notebooks and collections, nothing deeper.
  • Rich media walls. Images allowed, sparingly, pasted like a taped-in photo.
§ 03

The notebooks model

One book is the default. More are a choice, not a filing system.

You open the app to a shelf — a short row of spines. Most people keep one ("Journal"). Others separate a few streams: Journal, Work, Japan trip.

  • Each notebook has its own cover, paper and ribbons

    A work notebook can be plain black cloth with graph paper; the journal, oxblood leather with cream lined pages.

  • The daily page can span notebooks or stay separate

    A setting: "one shared today across all books" vs. "each book keeps its own days." Default is shared — you live one life.

  • Calendar and search are global

    They reach across the whole shelf. Collections stay inside their notebook unless you pin one to the shelf.

  • Archiving a full notebook

    When a book "fills up" (a soft page count, ~250 spreads) it can be closed and shelved — still searchable, no longer written in. A new volume opens: "Journal, Vol. 4."

§ 04

The margin assistant

You said you're open to it being visible. Here's a shape that stays in character.

It's not a chat bubble and not a robot. It's a pencil in the margin — a thin gutter on the right edge of the page where the app writes back to you in a lighter hand. You can write to it there too.

  • Ambient mode (default)

    It only writes unprompted for a few things: a rolled-too-many-times task, a scheduling clash, the weekly note. Everything it adds is erasable with one swipe.

  • Ask it directly

    Tap the margin and type: "when am I free for 2 hours this week?" / "pull every line tagged #book into a list." It answers in the margin, or lays the result onto a fresh page.

  • It drafts, you ink

    Anything it proposes appears in pencil-grey. It doesn't become "real" (dark ink) until you tap it. Nothing the assistant does is silent or final.

  • A dial, not a switch

    One control in settings — Off · Ambient · Active — rather than twenty toggles.

Privacy stance: journalling content is the most sensitive data a person owns. Assistant requests should be scoped to what you ask about, and the setting should make clear what leaves the device and what doesn't.

§ 05

Themes & materials

Set per notebook, never one global skin. The palette below is this brief's own — a starting point, not a spec.

Margin rule#B8544E

Covers

Leather colourways — oxblood, tan, cognac, forest, black — plus cloth and linen. One "worn-in" option that ages with use: the corners soften, the spine creases where you hold it.

Paper stock

Cream lined, white dot-grid, blank, graph, and a thin Tomoe-River-style sheet where ink shows through faintly from the previous page. Chosen per notebook.

Ink & tools

Fountain pen, ballpoint, pencil, felt-tip. Affects stroke weight and how your typed text is rendered — a chosen hand or a clean book serif.

Ribbons, bands & edges

Bookmark ribbon colours, an elastic closure that actually stretches shut, optional gilded page edges that catch the light.

Ambient

The cover picks up light based on time of day and weather. Subtle, and switchable off.

§ 06

UI & interaction

  • Open the cover to enter

    Close the elastic band to leave — which auto-saves. No "save" button anywhere.

  • Page flip follows your finger

    Weight and physics in the turn, a soft paper sound you can mute. Fast double-flip to riffle through a range; ribbons to jump instantly.

  • Typing is first-class on every device

    Your text renders in the notebook's chosen hand. On iPad, a keyboard case feels native; the Pencil is a later addition, not a v1 requirement.

  • Everything a real notebook pre-prints, this one does too

    Page numbers, an index at the front, date stamps, month tabs. The structure should feel manufactured, not generated.

  • The margin rule is load-bearing

    That red vertical line is where the calendar timeline, time-blocks and the assistant all live. It's the app's one piece of chrome.

Motion budget is small on purpose. The page turn is the one rich animation. Everything else — checking a task, filing a collection — is quick and quiet, so the flip stays special.

§ 07

Devices & sync

iPhone, iPad and PC, all in step. This choice shapes the whole codebase, so it's worth settling early.

ApproachWhy it fitsCost
Flutter One codebase for iOS, iPadOS and Windows. Full control of custom rendering and page physics. Real desktop support, not an afterthought. Custom text editing is fiddly; smaller hiring pool.
Web core + wrappers Canvas/WebGL page engine, then Capacitor for iOS and Tauri for Windows. Fastest to a prototype; one language. Buttery 60fps page physics is harder to guarantee across old devices.
Native + separate PC app SwiftUI gives the best possible feel on Apple hardware. You build the Windows app twice-over and still need a sync layer.

Sync & storage

  • Local-first, with CRDTs

    Every device holds the whole notebook and works fully offline. Edits merge without conflicts (Automerge or Yjs). Opening a page is instant because it's already there.

  • A thin sync relay

    A small server (or iCloud plus a relay for Windows) just passes encrypted change-sets between your devices. It never needs to understand them.

  • End-to-end encryption, non-negotiable

    The relay stores ciphertext. Losing all devices means a recovery key, kept by you. Nobody operating the service can read a page.

  • Export is a first-class feature

    A notebook can be dumped to PDF (as spreads) and to plain Markdown, any time. Your writing is never trapped.

§ 08

Open decisions

What to settle next, roughly in order.

  1. How visible is the assistant, exactly — is the right-margin "pencil" the model, or something even quieter?
  2. Shared daily page across all notebooks, or one per book? (Brief assumes shared.)
  3. Flutter vs. web-core — worth a weekend prototype of the page-flip in each before committing?
  4. What's the v1 platform cut? All three at launch, or iPhone + PC first and iPad close behind?
  5. Is there a subscription (for the sync relay + assistant), a one-time price, or both?
  6. Handwriting / Apple Pencil — explicitly v2, or a small v1 nod?
  7. Name: keep Marginalia, or keep brainstorming?
